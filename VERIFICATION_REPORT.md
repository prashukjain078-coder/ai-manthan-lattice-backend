## 7. Model Limits and Physical Scope Boundaries
To prevent misapplication in downstream aerospace or structural components, the following physical boundaries must be respected:
1. **Linear Elastic Small-Strain Regime:** Displacements and rotations are assumed small. Plastic hardening, large geometric non-linearities, and contact mechanics are not modeled.
2. **Slender Euler-Bernoulli Kinematics:** Valid for strut aspect ratios $r/L \le 0.15$. For stocky struts ($r/L > 0.20$), Timoshenko shear deformation and 3D nodal mass overlap effects become significant.
3. **First-Failure Strength Only:** Strength predictions represent the initial onset of yielding (von Mises) or strut-level elastic buckling (pinned-pinned Euler mode). They do not predict post-buckling load carrying or ultimate energy absorption plateau curves.
4. **No Thermal or Fatigue Coupling:** Creep, high-cycle fatigue, and temperature-dependent modulus reductions are outside current model scope.
5. **No Specimen-Level Global Buckling:** The solver evaluates homogenization of an infinite unit cell. Global Euler buckling of tall lattice columns depends on macro-boundary conditions and is not captured.
6. **No "Magic" Physics:** A lattice can **never** exceed the specific uniaxial stiffness of solid parent material ($E / \rho \le E_s$). The engineering utility of lattices lies in **tailored multidirectional stiffness, high second moment of area in sandwich panels, controlled impact crush absorption, and thermal/acoustic multifunctionality**.
